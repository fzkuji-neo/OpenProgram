Center canvas layout

Chinese specification and interactive prototype

Each layout occupies one top-level tab. Its serializable n-ary tree contains row and column splits with normalized weights and pane leaves referencing existing tabs. Existing two-pane groups migrate to row splits with their saved ratio. Content appears once within a layout.

Interaction

The canvas has no persistent layout toolbar. Each pane has a "•••" grip at the top centre with an 88×22px hit area: drag it to move the pane; click it, or rest the pointer on it as on the macOS green button, to open the tiling menu. A menu opened by hovering closes when the pointer leaves it or a drag starts. Pressing the grip never starts a text selection in the pane.

Drops follow macOS window tiling. The canvas edge band (40–96px, 14% of the short side, wide enough to hit without aiming) takes a half of the whole canvas on the left, right or bottom, and the other panes keep their arrangement in the rest. A corner takes a quarter by splitting the pane that holds it; when that pane is wider and taller than half, the remaining quarter on that side becomes an empty pane that offers free tabs. The top edge is Fill: a dragged pane leaves the split and opens on its own, and a dragged tab simply opens. Inside a pane, the left and right quarters split it sideways and the middle column splits it above or below by its half, so no part of a pane is dead and the lower middle always means below. Nearest-edge triangles are avoided because left and bottom are hard to tell apart near a corner. Holding Option anywhere in a pane replaces it, and the replaced content returns to the strip. An empty pane accepts a drop anywhere. The preview shows the exact rect the tab will take, computed with the layout code, and names the destination. Pressing or releasing Option during a drag updates the preview immediately. Drag a pane back to the strip to detach it; desktop transfers retain layout metadata. Closing a pane returns its content to the strip without closing the session.

The tiling menu mirrors Window ▸ Move & Resize and Arrange with small layout glyphs, and a hint line names the glyph under the pointer and its shortcut. Move and resize applies to this pane: left, right, top and bottom halves, Fill, and the four quarters. A pane sent to a half leaves the others in their order in the other half; a quarter fully held by another pane trades places. Arrange lays out the whole canvas as left and right, top and bottom, left and stacked right, or quarters, with this pane first. Contents fill presets in reading order; overflow returns to the strip and spare panes stay empty. Below these are Return to previous layout, Equalize sizes, More layouts (single, nine, sixteen, saved layouts and Save layout…), and Close pane.

Return to previous layout first leaves Fill, then steps back past the last tile, arrange, drop, equalize, close or divider drag. Entries are found by any tab or group they involved, so a pane pulled out of the split can be put back.

Shortcuts use Control + Option because Fn + Control belongs to macOS: arrows tile the focused pane to a half; adding Shift arranges left and right or top and bottom with the focused pane on the arrow's side; F toggles Fill; R returns to the previous layout. Command/Control + Backslash splits right and adding Shift splits below. Command/Control + Shift + Enter toggles Fill. Command/Control + W closes the focused pane in a multi-pane layout. Alt + Command/Control + arrow moves spatial focus.

Drag separators to resize. Within 10px of a third, a half or two thirds of their split they stick and show the ratio. Double-click equalizes the adjacent pair; arrow keys on a focused separator move it by 2%.

The layout tab in the strip shows the first two pane titles. They share its width by length: a short title stays whole and the longer one takes the rest before it truncates, each keeping at least 56px. More than two members add a "+N" count.

Drag lifecycle

The canvas will host many small app windows, so every drag must end cleanly and leave other panes alone. A pane drag's release, Escape, pointercancel, window blur and any error while resolving the drop all run one cleanup exactly once: the floating label and drop cue go, the grip returns, pointer capture is released and data-canvas-dragging is cleared. A failed drop lookup counts as no drop.

A release over the window-drag strip, a native web view or another app never reaches the page. Every drag (tab, pane, divider) registers its cancel and release with setCanvasDragging(true, owner); the first pointermove with no button down is treated as the release, and a new press while a drag is still open cancels the old one. While a drag runs, nothing in the window is a -webkit-app-region: drag region.

The dragged pane carries a data-pane-dragging attribute that hides its grip through CSS, never the grip's inline style, and a new drag clears any stale one. Pressing a grip only prepares a drag: no store commit, focus or route change, and no text selection underneath; a click focuses. Divider drags repaint at most once per frame through previewCanvas, which changes only the in-memory tree, and commit once with setCanvas on release. A native web view that must freeze is captured first and hidden when the snapshot arrives, or after 160ms, so it never flashes a paused card. The drop cue has no backdrop blur and moves with a 120ms ease-out, without overshoot.

Rendering and native views

Recursive geometry calculation feeds stable content hosts. Tree edits and zoom do not reparent content components. Zoom hides sibling panes. Tiny panes show their title and type with a zoom action. Native web views report individual bounds. During dragging, undersized presentation or beyond the eight-visible-view allowance, they show captured images. Selecting a paused view gives it priority. Hidden native views remain alive; this is not process termination.

Persistence and boundaries

The existing window tab payload stores each group's canvas tree, focus and zoom. Missing content references become empty panes. Cross-window payload validation bounds tree depth, node count, and serialized size independently of the normal layout editor. Resources attachment continues to use its existing backend ownership operation.

Implementation status

The tree, store actions, canvas controls, drop presentation, snapshot presentation and persistence are implemented, including macOS-style edge and corner tiling, the hover tiling menu, Control + Option shortcuts, divider snapping and length-weighted layout tab titles. The interactive prototype linked from the Chinese specification still demonstrates the earlier centre replace and toolbar; the text is authoritative.

Dragging a pane into the top tab strip shows the same blue rounded drop preview as canvas docking. The cue follows the actual tab-strip bounds and disappears when leaving or cancelling; releasing inside those bounds returns the pane to a standalone tab.
